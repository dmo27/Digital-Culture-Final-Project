About Our Project


"Facebook is for boomers!"

You have probably heard this comment being made thousands of times by digital natives as they doomscroll on TikTok or Instagram; surely the correct, fashionable social media choices. But is this statement inherently true? Are different age groups really attached to certain platforms or is this merely an outdated stereotype in an age of unprecedented technological plurality? This is what our team wanted to find out by examining how different generations use social media in their daily lives.








Why This Topic


How are intergenerational relations expressed through platform usage and migration?

Initially, our team wanted to focus on how social media algorithms act depending on the user's attributes (i.e. gender, hobbies etc.). However, we soon realised that tracing this phenomenon constitutes a hefty task due to the overwhelming amount of elements that come into play and the lack of efficient research methodology. Thus, we narrowed our scope to a single key factor, age, and we started to ask ourselves how different generations interact with and on these platforms. What role does social media play in daily life? Are different platforms used as a means of expression? Is there an observable generation gap? These are all sub-questions that birthed our research question above.
